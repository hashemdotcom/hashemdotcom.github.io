MSc dissertation / University of Birmingham

Enhancing surface material recognition in virtual reality

How do visual, auditory, and tactile cues work together when people identify materials in VR? I built a controlled multisensory experiment and compared recognition accuracy, confidence, and patterns of error across seven cue combinations.

The question

Does adding more senses always improve recognition?

Participants identified wood, sandpaper, metal, or fabric after a guided finger sweep in VR. The experiment tested vision, audio, and vibrotactile feedback alone and in combination, then compared accuracy, confidence, confusion patterns, and movement.

The study was designed to test whether richer combinations consistently helped, or whether cue quality and alignment mattered more than the number of channels.

What I found

Vision dominated this task

Accuracy stayed near ceiling when vision was present. Non-visual conditions were weaker, and combining cues did not automatically improve performance.

More cues were not always better

Adding audio or tactile feedback to vision offered little additional benefit in this experiment.

Confidence usually tracked accuracy

Participants tended to be more confident when correct, though high-confidence errors appeared under cue conflict.

Some materials were harder to tell apart

Metal was identified most accurately; errors were more common among wood, fabric, and sandpaper.

Contribution and next steps

Design for cue quality and alignment

The project brings seven visual, auditory, and tactile configurations into one VR protocol and combines recognition accuracy with confidence, error structure, and movement measures. The results suggest that XR feedback should be selected and aligned for its diagnostic value, rather than added simply to increase the number of sensations.

The controlled renderings and guided sweep may not capture natural surface exploration. Future work could tune cross-modal timing and intensity, test other cue mappings, and use more naturalistic interactions.
